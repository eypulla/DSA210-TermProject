Motivation

Why volleyball, and why economics?

Volleyball is a sport I closely follow. While watching international competitions, I noticed that certain countries consistently dominate — but not always the wealthiest ones. Brazil and Turkey produce world-class teams despite having lower GDP per capita than many European nations, while massive populous countries are often absent from the top rankings. This sparked my central question: what actually explains national volleyball success?

Turkey is a particularly interesting case. I grew up watching Vakıfbank and Fenerbahçe dominate European club volleyball, and the national women's team consistently finishing in the top four at World Championships — ahead of countries with GDP per capita two or three times higher. That gap between economic indicators and volleyball results was what originally made me want to look at this properly.

Who Are the Top Performing Nations?

European nations dominate both men's and women's rankings. Brazil and Japan are the only non-European countries consistently in the global top 5–6. The US ranks 3rd in men's but only 7th in women's, while Turkey sits 17th in men's but 4th in women's — a striking gender gap explored further below.

Do GDP and HDI Predict Volleyball Success?

Both log GDP and HDI show a positive association with volleyball performance. HDI appears to be the stronger predictor — the points cluster more tightly around its regression line. Some countries sit far above the line (Brazil, Turkey) while some wealthy nations fall well below it.

Log GDP and HDI vs women's WR points. Regression lines shown.
3

Correlation Analysis

The Spearman correlation matrix confirms the visual impression. HDI has the strongest correlation with volleyball points. Log population has near-zero correlation — the population paradox. Men's and women's points correlate strongly with each other (r ≈ 0.7), suggesting shared country-level factors drive success across both genders.

Spearman correlation matrix — all key variables.

Which Countries Punch Above Their Weight?

After fitting a regression model (Log GDP → Women's WR points), the residuals reveal which countries perform far above or below their economic expectations. Brazil and Turkey are the biggest over-performers — wealth helps, but it is not the whole story.

The Population Paradox & Continental Patterns

There is essentially no relationship between population size and volleyball success — the regression line is nearly flat. Slovenia (≈2.1M people) consistently outranks countries with populations 50–100 times larger. CEV (Europe) has the highest median performance and the greatest spread.

Left: population vs avg WR points. Right: performance by confederation.
6

Gender Gap

Turkey and Brazil have strongly positive gaps — their women's programs considerably outperform their men's. France and Slovenia show the reverse. The HDI–gender gap relationship is weak, suggesting the gap is driven more by country-specific volleyball culture than development levels.

Conclusions

Four things the data tells us

1
Development Over Pure Wealth
HDI consistently outperforms GDP as a predictor. Volleyball excellence is linked to broad societal well-being — health, education, and equality — more than raw economic output. The HDI effect is notably larger for women's teams.
2
The Population Paradox
No significant evidence that larger population leads to better results. Slovenia (≈2.1M people) consistently outranks countries 50–100× larger. The quality of the athletic pipeline matters far more than the talent pool size.
3
The "Volleyball Culture" Factor
Brazil and Turkey are the most significant positive outliers across all models. Their success far exceeds what economic indicators predict — pointing to federation investment, historical tradition, and the cultural weight of volleyball.
4
Predicting Groups, Not Exact Numbers
Regression R² is low, but confederation classification achieves 56% accuracy vs a 25% baseline. Economic profiles carry real signal about volleyball success patterns — just not enough to pin down exact rankings.
